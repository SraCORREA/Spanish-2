🕯️ Room 1: Setting the scene

Setting the scene

Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead) is a celebration from Mexico. Families remember and honor their ancestors (los antepasados): grandparents, parents, and friends who have died. It is a happy, colorful tradition, not a scary one.

The celebration happens on November 1 and November 2. November 1 is for children who have died (el Día de los Angelitos, the day of the little angels). November 2 is for adults (el Día de los Difuntos).

Día de los Muertos is not “Mexican Halloween.” Halloween is about costumes and fear. Día de los Muertos is about memory, family, and love. The idea is that the spirits of the family come back for one night to visit, so the family prepares a party for them.

The tradition is a mix of two cultures. The Indigenous people of Mexico, like the Aztecs (also called the Mexica), celebrated their dead for centuries. Then, in the 1500s, the Spanish brought the Catholic holidays of All Saints' Day (November 1) and All Souls' Day (November 2). The two traditions combined into one celebration.

In 2008, UNESCO (a part of the United Nations) put Día de los Muertos on its list of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. That means the whole world recognizes it as an important cultural tradition. Today it is celebrated in Mexico, in many parts of Latin America, and in cities in the United States with big Mexican-American communities.

🔗 Connection: history → vocabularyThe word muerto means “dead” and día means “day”. Many words in this unit are cognates: words that look like English. Celebración = celebration, tradición = tradition, familia = family, memoria = memory, espíritu = spirit.

🎨 Room 2: Art and symbols

Art and symbolism: the ofrenda

The center of the celebration is the ofrenda, also called the altar. An ofrenda is a table with levels (steps) where the family puts objects for the spirits. Every object is a symbol: it has a special meaning.

La foto (the photo): a photo of the person who died goes at the top of the altar. The altar is for that person. The family also adds things the person liked: their favorite food, music, or a hobby.

Las velas (the candles): the light of the candles guides the spirits home. Each candle can represent one person the family remembers.

El cempasúchil (the marigold): a bright orange and yellow flower. Its strong color and smell make a path so the spirits can find the altar. People call it la flor de muerto, the flower of the dead.

El papel picado (cut tissue paper): colorful paper with designs cut into it. It moves with the wind and represents the wind and how fragile (delicate) life is.

Las calaveras de azúcar (sugar skulls): skulls made of sugar, decorated with bright colors and sometimes a name. They are not scary. In this tradition the skull is a happy symbol: it reminds us that death is a natural part of life.

El pan de muerto (bread of the dead): a sweet, round bread with pieces on top shaped like bones. It is food for the spirits and for the family.

El agua y la sal (water and salt): a glass of water for the spirits, who are thirsty after their long trip. Salt purifies (cleans) the spirit.

Art: La Catrina

The most famous image of Día de los Muertos is La Catrina: an elegant skeleton lady with a fancy hat. The Mexican artist José Guadalupe Posada drew her around 1910. His message was a joke with a lesson: rich or poor, elegant or simple, everyone is equal in the end. Later, the painter Diego Rivera put her in a famous mural and gave her the name Catrina.

🔗 Connection: art → grammarLook at the words for the objects: la foto, la vela, la flor, el pan, el papel. In Spanish every noun has a gender: la (feminine) or el (masculine). You will use these nouns with the verbs hay (there is / there are) and tener (to have): En el altar hay velas. La familia tiene una foto.

⚜️ Room 3: Louisiana connection

A Louisiana connection: All Saints' Day in New Orleans

You do not have to travel to Mexico to see families honoring their dead. In New Orleans, November 1 is All Saints' Day (la Toussaint in French). It is a Catholic tradition that came with the French and Spanish settlers of Louisiana, the same Catholic holiday that mixed into Día de los Muertos in Mexico.

On All Saints' Day, New Orleans families go to the cemetery. They clean and whitewash (paint white) the family tomb, and they decorate it with flowers, especially chrysanthemums. Many families bring a picnic and spend the afternoon together, telling stories about the relatives who are buried there.

New Orleans cemeteries are famous because the tombs are built above the ground. The city is very low and wet, so the tombs look like small white houses. People call them cities of the dead. The oldest one, St. Louis Cemetery No. 1, opened in 1789, when Louisiana was a Spanish colony.

Today New Orleans also has a growing Latino community, and every year there are Día de los Muertos events with altars, music, and face painting like a calavera. The two traditions live side by side in the same city: flowers on the tomb on November 1, and an ofrenda at home on November 2.

🔗 Connection: Louisiana → historyBoth traditions come from the same Catholic holidays, All Saints' Day and All Souls' Day. Spain ruled Louisiana from 1763 to 1800 and ruled Mexico for 300 years. That is why New Orleans and Mexico share so much: Catholic faith, Spanish words, and the idea of honoring the family dead.

📖 Room 4: La mosca y el altar

📖 Reading tipsLook for cognates first: familia, especial, activa, niveles, foto, música, chocolate, colores, sorpresa, delicioso, celebración, tradición. Then look for the verbs you know: es, está, tiene, hay, le gusta, vuelo, quiero, prefiero, me paro, mira, dice.
🪰 La mosca y el altar

Hola. Soy Lola, la mosca. Vivo en una casa grande en Nueva Orleans con mi familia: la mujer, el hombre, el perro Max y el gato.

Hoy es el 1 de noviembre. La familia está muy activa. La mujer tiene una mesa especial con tres niveles. ¡Es un altar! Es una ofrenda para la abuela Rosa.

En el altar hay una foto de la abuela. La abuela es una mujer con una sonrisa grande. La mujer dice: «A la abuela le gusta la música y le gusta el chocolate».

El hombre pone papel picado de colores: rosa, amarillo y azul. El papel se mueve con el viento. El perro Max mira las flores de cempasúchil. Son naranjas y amarillas. ¡Tienen un olor muy fuerte!

Yo vuelo por la cocina. ¡Qué sorpresa! Hay pan de muerto en la mesa. Es un pan dulce y delicioso. Quiero el pan. Prefiero el pan a las flores.

Me paro en el pan. La mujer me mira y dice: «¡No, mosca! El pan es para la abuela». Pero no está enojada. Está contenta. Toda la familia está contenta. No es un día triste. Es una celebración.

Por la noche, la familia enciende las velas. La luz es bonita. La mujer habla de la abuela y todos sonríen. Yo pienso: «Me gusta esta tradición. La familia recuerda a la abuela con amor». Y yo también soy parte de la familia.

Comprehension

Here is a quick summary in English to check your reading: The altar is for the grandmother, abuela Rosa. Her photo is on the altar. The grandmother likes music and chocolate. The man puts up papel picado in pink, yellow and blue. The dog looks at the orange and yellow marigolds. Lola the fly wants the pan de muerto and lands on it. The woman says no, but she is happy, not angry. At night the family lights the candles and remembers the grandmother with love.

🔗 Connection: story → grammarThe story uses the 4 big verbs: soy / es (ser), está / están (estar), tiene / tienen (tener), le gusta (gustar). It also uses boot verbs: vuelo (volar, o→ue), quiero (querer, e→ie), prefiero (preferir, e→ie), se mueve (mover, o→ue), pienso (pensar, e→ie). You will practice all of them in Lesson 2.

🌼 Room 5: Mi altar

Mi altar: our class ofrenda

Our class is building an ofrenda too. Every student brings one object. Think about what you brought and why. Here are the Spanish words you need: Yo traigo… (I bring…), Me gusta… (I like…), Mi parte favorita es… (My favorite part is…), porque (because), bonito/a (pretty), importante, especial, los colores.

Example: Yo traigo papel picado de color rosa. Me gusta porque es bonito y representa el viento. Mi parte favorita del altar es la foto, porque es para una persona especial.

Remember: an ofrenda is a gift. The objects are for the person the family remembers. Each object you bring is one symbol of love and memory. There are no wrong objects: a flower, a photo, a drawing, a favorite candy, a candle, or a paper decoration all belong on the altar.
